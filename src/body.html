How to read these columns

vs city avg compares each district with the average district citywide. vs boro avg compares it with the average district in its own borough (shown while districts stay inside boroughs).

Race uses six race/ethnicity groups. Econ uses students in and not in poverty. All four columns run from 0 (fully integrated) upward; higher means more segregated.

Race H, Econ H
Theil's H for the schools inside the district, from 0 to 1. 0 = every school has the district's overall mix; 1 = each school serves only one group. The City row shows the enrollment-weighted average across districts.
Race vs city, Econ vs city
How far the district's mix is from the city's. 0 = same mix as the city (74% poverty for econ); higher = further away. Usually below 1, but a district made up mostly of one small group can go higher. The City row is the plan's Theil's H across districts, which is the weighted average of these values, from 0 to 1.

How it works

You can build districts from four kinds of building blocks (Plan tab): DOE elementary school zones, DOE middle school zones (elementary zones in districts with middle school choice), 2020 Census tracts, or school areas (the city blocks in each district nearest each school building, so their edges follow streets). Every kind is split at the official district lines. The description below uses elementary zones.

The map is built from DOE's elementary school attendance zones (2024–25). Where a zone crosses a community district line it is split at the official DCP line, so today's districts match the official map exactly. Land outside every zone (parks, marshes) is joined to the next zone in the same district. Districts are made by moving whole zones; every school inside a zone moves with it.

A few schools report to a different district than the one they sit in, such as Beacon High School (District 3, located in District 2). They don't change the map. By default they are counted in the district they report to. Once you move the zone they sit in, they move with that zone.

The scorecard compares your plan with today's districts, counting the same schools:

  • Largest gap from citywide average: how far the biggest or smallest district is from the average district citywide (counted students ÷ number of districts).
  • Largest gap within a borough: the same, compared with the average district in its own borough. Staten Island's single district is always 0% here, so use the citywide figure to compare across boroughs.
  • Compactness: average Polsby-Popper score (1 = a circle). Shorelines lower every score, so compare plans with each other.
  • Racial/ethnic segregation: Theil's H across districts (0 = every district matches the city mix).
  • Race H and Econ H (Districts tab): Theil's H across the schools inside each district, from 0 (every school has the district's mix) to 1 (each school serves one group).
  • Race vs city and Econ vs city (Districts tab): how far each district's mix is from the city's (0 = same). Their enrollment-weighted average is the plan's Theil's H shown on the scorecard.
  • Economic segregation: Theil's H for students in and not in poverty (0 = every district has the city's poverty rate).
  • Students moved: share of counted students whose school would be in a different district number than today.

Data

  • Schools, buildings, addresses, superintendents: NYC DOE LCGMS report, pulled .
  • Enrollment and demographics: NYC DOE Demographic Snapshot, .
  • Building locations: LCGMS addresses geocoded with NYC Planning's GeoSearch, checked against each school's geographic district.
  • Zones: NYC Open Data School Zones 2024–2025 (Elementary School).
  • School areas: NYC Open Data 2020 Census Blocks.
  • District lines: NYC Open Data School Districts (DCP).

Limits

  • District 79 programs are not in the public LCGMS report, so they can't be placed on the map.
  • Demographics are reported per school, so each school counts with its whole enrollment.
  • High school superintendent schools have a district number but admit students citywide.
  • Poverty values reported as "Above 95%" are counted as 97%.
